# Screenwriting and Dramaturgy

Protect the author's intent. Diagnose the dramatic problem before rewriting, and prefer the smallest change that solves it.

## Establish the source of truth

- Identify the current approved script or scene version before comparing or editing.
- Separate approved decisions from alternatives, open questions, and rejected ideas.
- Do not reopen a settled choice without new evidence or a contradiction in the current draft.
- When the source is unavailable or versions conflict, state exactly what is missing instead of inventing continuity.

## Analyze the scene

Determine:

- the scene's dramatic function;
- whose scene it is and what that character wants now;
- the obstacle, pressure, or mismatch preventing an immediate result;
- what changes between entrance and exit;
- the audience's information, expectation, and emotional position;
- the scene's causal link to the previous and following scenes;
- whether dialogue, action, sound, image, or omission carries each beat.

Treat silence, routine actions, objects, spatial relationships, and repeated behavior as dramatic material. Do not add exposition when the audience can infer the fact from behavior or contrast.

## Audit before rewriting

For a full audit, provide:

1. A concise verdict on whether the scene or script works as intended.
2. Scores only for relevant criteria: premise, character, conflict, structure, visual storytelling, subtext, rhythm, emotional effect, ending, and production clarity.
3. Concrete strengths tied to moments in the text.
4. Risks ordered by impact, not by ease of correction.
5. A short revision priority list.

Label uncertain reasoning as a hypothesis. Distinguish an observed textual fact from an interpretation or proposal.

## Revise locally

- Preserve working beats, tone, restraint, and thematic ambiguity.
- Change only what is needed to solve the diagnosed problem.
- Prefer playable actions to explanatory dialogue.
- Check setup/payoff, entrances and exits, object continuity, knowledge state, and emotional progression after every change.
- Avoid melodramatic emphasis when the film's language is observational or restrained.
- Do not make symbolism announce itself. Let recurrence, contrast, and placement carry meaning.

When offering alternatives, explain the dramatic tradeoff of each. Recommend one option when the evidence supports it.

## Project convention: “Люда”

When working on the short film “Люда,” preserve the latest approved choices unless the user changes them:

- The present-day cane is visible without emphasis; it is absent from the basketball memory.
- The film does not show or explain the injury.
- The basketball passage conveys speed, height, youth, and belonging rather than tragedy.
- A continuing dribble ends with a third bounce that cuts by sound and motion to a package of meat placed near Люда on the conveyor.
- After the man's departure, Люда continues working before the internal shift becomes visible.
- The final direct look and sincere “Здравствуйте” address the viewer as the next customer.
- Do not restore the removed closing line “Хорошего дня” unless explicitly requested.

## Handoff

After an approved revision, summarize only production-relevant consequences: new action, timing, prop, sound cue, continuity condition, or required shot. Do not silently redesign camera blocking or generation prompts.
