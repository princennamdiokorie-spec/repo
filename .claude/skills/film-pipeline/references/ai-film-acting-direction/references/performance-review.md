# Take review and examples

## Review

1. Read the brief and inspect the supplied take. Cite timecode or an observed range where possible. If there is only a still, describe what it shows and keep motion, audio and causality provisional. A silent file cannot establish delivery or lip sync by ear.
2. Compare intended trigger, gaze target, delay, intensity and landing against what is actually visible or audible. Check that framing, focus, crop and occlusion allow the acting to read. Separate observed behavior from a suspected cause.
3. If the take already communicates the intended beat, preserve it. If it diverges, locate the first useful change. Choose between a local prompt or input adjustment, a viable trim, a reframing proposal or a new take as the material permits. Change one variable per diagnostic pass when feasible. State what to inspect in the next result.
4. Check the adjacent cut if supplied. Compare where the character looks, what their hands hold, the motion phase, whether the reaction has already played, and what the audience currently knows.

## Hypothetical naturalistic performance

A cashier recognizes a customer by voice but wants the transaction to stay ordinary. The voice lands while her hand is moving toward the scanner. Her hand pauses briefly; her gaze finds the speaker; she resumes the scan. Use a medium close-up that can read her eyes and hand together if framing permits. A return to routine carries the attempted concealment. Treat this sequence as an illustration, not a decision for LUDA.

## Hypothetical take repair

A character is meant to notice the person to her right only after an off-screen sound. The take begins with her already turned right. If verified in the actual frames, the earliest issue is the initial gaze. Keep the approved framing and direct a clear initial target, then cue the gaze shift after the sound. Use screen direction for composition and anatomical direction for the character; establish their relationship from the approved camera. A successful retry shows both gaze states in the correct order and preserves the final eye-line.
