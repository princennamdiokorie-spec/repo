# Актёрская режиссура для ИИ-кино

Use the current scene, approved decisions and references. Protect locked story facts, dialogue, geography, character design, reveal order and camera. Assign authority by property: spatial references govern positions and timing, and identity references govern appearance. Distinguish observed facts, approved decisions and suggested interpretations. If information is missing, proceed with a reversible proposal and state the assumption. Ask only about a choice that materially changes the result. The director approves creative changes and launches paid generation.

## Choose the requested work

- **Stage a scene:** establish its dramatic change, principal objectives, beats, physical staging, audience point of view and needed coverage.
- **Direct a performance:** map what the character wants, hides and reveals into visible and audible actions.
- **Review a take:** inspect available frames or video and audio, identify the first observable divergence, and propose a minimal fix. Read [review and examples](references/performance-review.md) for take diagnosis or a worked model.
- **Write a prompt:** turn the chosen performance into one self-contained set of observable instructions, using the selected generator's prompt guidance and the user's reference map.

Combine modes when the request calls for it. Respond in the user's language. Match the existing prompt and dialogue languages when writing a prompt.

## Dramatic action and staging

1. Determine what changes, whose experience leads, who knows what, and when the viewer should learn it. Preserve intended ambiguity.
2. For each relevant character identify objective, motive, obstacle and tactic from the material. Express the tactic as a playable action toward the partner or situation, such as test, deflect, contain, reassure or stall. Mark interpretations as proposals.
3. Break at changes in information, tactic, relationship or control. For each beat record the trigger, the character's perceptible response, partner reaction if needed, and what carries into the next beat. Decide whose reaction the audience sees and whether it lands before, during or after the line or cut.
4. Stage from the objective and space. Track orientation, marks, eyelines, screen direction, entrances/exits and prop contact. If a plausible everyday action already exists, let the actor continue, interrupt or resume it as the scene requires. A new gesture, prop or task belongs in a proposal until confirmed when it affects canon or staging. Preserve intentional stillness and use only the signs of attention the shot needs.

## From inner beat to observable performance

For each important beat identify what the actor experiences, what they try to show or suppress, and what slips through. Choose one primary channel and one supporting detail if useful. Work from gaze targets and timing, a brief halt or breath change, jaw or eyelid tension, a change in posture, a hand action, or vocal delivery. Coordinate channels in order. Avoid stacking facial cues or prescribing a universal body-language meaning.

Calibrate intensity to the project. For naturalism use minimal, subtle, clear or high as working language and start with the smallest readable signal. For stylized animation use the approved register and use the bundled [anime emotion staging guide](../anime-emotion-staging/guide.md) for anime performance and graphic conventions. With dialogue specify speaker, pause, tempo, volume, articulation or emphasis only where useful. Preserve exact lines. In a silent beat, use room sound or breath if it matters. An off-screen trigger needs only the identity the scene establishes.

## Camera for the performance

Choose what must read and when the viewer notices it. A wide can carry distance and body language. A medium can preserve hands and partner relation. A medium close-up can show breath and the eyes. A close-up fits a facial shift that bears the beat. Keep face and hands within the usable focus zone when both carry information. No extra coverage by habit.

Inherit approved optics and camera position. When proposing new optics, consider distance, sensor, perspective and shot size together. Full-frame-equivalent starting points when useful: 24–35 mm for context, 40–58 mm for grounded dialogue, 65–85 mm for restrained face work, 100 mm+ for deliberate isolation. Prefer readable eyes and natural facial perspective over default shallow focus. Explain the event that justifies a cut or camera movement. Keep axis and sightlines consistent. Route a full camera plan to the cinematography workflow and cut decisions to the editing workflow when needed.

## Handoff and checks

For a scene plan provide only the detail needed: beat/trigger, playable task, observable action and response, spatial/gaze target, audible cue, camera intent and landing. For a prompt include start state, ordered actions, reaction delay where needed, on-screen direction, camera relation and final state. Keep exact reference labels and bind each asset to its job. Separate the explanation of intent from the physical instructions the generator should render.

Check that the actor can perceive each cue, the intended signal is visible at the chosen scale, and silence or voice fits the action. At joins carry gaze, body and prop position, action and breath phase, intensity and whether the reaction already happened. Preserve both emotional and physical continuity. For a local repair identify the smallest change to try and what visible result would count as success.
