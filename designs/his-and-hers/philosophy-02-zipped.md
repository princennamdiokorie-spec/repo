# Zipped

*A design philosophy*

Zipped believes the most honest symbol of togetherness is a working mechanism. Two separate halves, each complete on its own, are joined tooth by tooth into one structure that can open and close again. The work never pictures a union; it builds the joint itself. The eye sees a single form, then notices it is made of two, and then sees exactly where they meet.

Colour is borrowed from the wardrobe and the workbench. One half is faded denim, the colour of a pair of jeans after ten years of wear; the other is terracotta, a warm clay red. Where the two must agree, the shared hardware is deep ink. The ground is unbleached cotton. Every hue is flat and matte, and each one earns its place, meticulously calibrated so that the two halves feel equal in weight even though their colours are different.

The geometry is industrial and tactile. There are thick vertical rails, small rounded teeth that alternate left and right in a strict rhythm, and a slider with a pull tab that looks as if a thumb could catch it. Everything sits on a quiet grid, and every radius is chosen with the painstaking attention of someone who has taken many zippers apart to learn how they are made. The mark should feel engineered and friendly at the same time.

Scale proves the idea. The mark is shown large, where every tooth can be counted, then smaller and smaller until the teeth blur into a solid bar and the letterform simply remains. That progression is presented deliberately, as evidence of master-level execution: a form that has detail when you look closely and stays clear from far away.

Type is confident, lowercase, and warm: a heavy grotesque with tight spacing, as if printed on a label stitched into a collar. One serif italic ampersand stays expressive. Clinical notes sit at the edges in a small mono face, like part numbers on a technical drawing. Words are few. Each element, whether rail, tooth, label or margin, is placed with the care of countless hours of refinement, the product of deep expertise.
